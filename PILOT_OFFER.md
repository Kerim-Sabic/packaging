# PackProof — a decision you can audit

## Proposed first engagement

One retail category. Six weeks. Ten specifications, three alternatives and one trial plan. Procurement owns the decision; quality and operations own their release gates. This offer is a proposal for discussion, with no customer commitment or achieved result claimed.

**Proposed fixed pilot fee: €1,500, excluding applicable taxes.** This is an unvalidated starting price, not observed willingness to pay. Includes local setup, document review support, a comparison register, supplier questions, a cost/sensitivity worksheet and a handover. Laboratory testing, physical samples, travel and retail trial costs require a separate agreed budget. The present product is local and single-user.

## Deliverables and acceptance

| When | Deliverable | Proposed acceptance gate |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Ten permissioned specifications; expert reference fields; baseline review timing | Every accepted critical field links to a reviewed passage and exact product/version. Missing values remain explicit. |
| Weeks 3–4 | Three alternatives, complete pack scope, quotes and decision-changing questions | No unresolved critical use mismatch at trial entry. Compare equivalent capacity, protection, food and service conditions. |
| Weeks 5–6 | One owner-approved trial plan and decision brief | Agreed sample size, budget, acceptance limits and stop rules. Actual trial occurs only after approval and evidence completion. |

Software success target: at least 30% lower median review time on comparable tasks, with no increase in critical errors. This is a target to test, not a result. Reject unsupported accepted fields regardless of speed.

## Commercial test, with visible assumptions

Illustrative delivery budget: 24 hours × €35/hour = €840 direct labour; plus €150 allocated support/compute = €990. At the proposed €1,500 fee, contribution is €510 (34%) before acquisition, overhead, taxes and overruns. At 40 delivery hours the same assumptions leave a €50 loss. The pilot tests repeatability as well as willingness to pay.

Customer review benefit = comparable reviews per month × minutes saved per review ÷ 60 × fully loaded hourly cost. Illustrative example: 40 × 30 ÷ 60 × €35 = €700/month of staff time capacity. This is not cash savings unless work/cost is actually avoided. It is not measured benefit. At those assumptions, €1,500 equals about 2.1 months of that capacity value, before customer onboarding effort and ongoing fees. Packaging savings require a separate measured baseline and supplier quotes; do not add speculative material savings to sell the pilot.

After a successful pilot, test a proposed €300/month single-team support licence for the existing local workflow. This implies 8.6 hours/month of valued time at €35/hour to cover the fee alone. No shared cloud workspace or integration is included. Obtain a paid renewal before treating this as recurring demand. Price, scope and support load remain hypotheses.

## Evaluation that can survive questions

1. A packaging reviewer creates reference answers before model use. Record document date, exact SKU, component scope and missing fields.
2. Use comparable tasks in alternating manual/app order to reduce learning effects. Record active minutes and corrections separately; disclose small sample size.
3. Count accepted unsupported critical fields, incorrect units, wrong component scope and missed contradictions. Report counts and denominators, not a universal accuracy percentage.
4. Ask procurement, quality and operations to complete a comparison, find its source and create a next action without coaching. Log completion, errors and friction; do not invent feedback.
5. For retail trials, agree leakage, product loss, shelf life, handling time, transport and consumer criteria with the retailer. Hold safety, protection and food loss as release gates; stop on a critical failure.
